Approach

The order the analysis ran in

Data checks first, then the cost questions — so that anything found later rests on numbers that were verified rather than assumed.

  1. Check the sheets against each other Which vessels appear where, and whether the transaction detail adds up to the monthly summary.
  2. Investigate the off-budget columns The workbook never states whether unbudgeted spending sits inside the reported actuals or on top of them. Every conclusion about overspending depends on that answer.
  3. Compare years on the same months The data stops in July 2026, so every year was restricted to January–July before comparing — the final year is otherwise judged on seven months against twelve.
  4. Localise: by vessel, by category, then against deferred exposure From the fleet figure down to which vessels moved, which cost types moved, and how cost performance compares with the handover sheet.

Data findings

What the checks on the workbook itself showed

1 · Four vessels are missing from the handover sheet

Three sheets — Vessel_Master, Monthly_Budget_Actual and Cost_Transactions — all hold the same 14 vessels. Handover_Deferred_Risk holds only 10: V1002, V1007, V1012 and V1013 are absent.

That is a real gap, because deferred exposure is the value of work that should have been done but was not — arguably the single most important thing to know about a vessel being handed over.

A missing row can mean two opposite things: nothing has been deferred on that vessel, or nobody has assessed it yet. The sheet gives no way to tell which.

There is a reason to lean towards not assessed: all four vessels flagged Handover Review in the master sheet are present on the handover sheet, while all four missing vessels are Active.

Decision

These four vessels are excluded from any deferred-exposure comparison and reported as not assessed.

2 · The two spending sheets agree, apart from rounding

Adding up Amount_USD from the transactions and comparing against Actual_USD in the monthly sheet: all 3,472 rows matched on structure, with nothing orphaned on either side.

690 rows differed in amount by more than one cent — but the largest single difference was 0.02, and the total across every row was 10.28.

The pattern fits rounding and nothing else. If the two sheets genuinely disagreed about spending, the differences would vary in size.

3 · The curious case of Off_Budget_USD

Because the reconciliation above used all transactions regardless of budget status, it also establishes that non-budgeted spend sits inside Actual_USD.

Finding 1 — off-budget spending is inside the actuals. The two transactions add to 4,658.79, which is exactly Actual_USD. The non-budgeted transaction is one of the things being summed, and this holds across all 228 off-budget cells.

Finding 2 — the off-budget amount does not match its own records. The monthly sheet reports 115.07 of off-budget spending; the records show a single non-budgeted transaction of 1,259.88 — eleven times larger. This is not a one-off: across the fleet, non-budgeted transactions total 711,718.50 while the Off_Budget_USD column totals 106,513.80.

Both sheets flag the same 228 vessel-month-category cells, so they agree on where unplanned spending happened but highly disagree on how much was spent.

What else was checked

  • Non-budgeted transactions were expected to cluster on unplanned work like emergency crew travel. They do not.
  • Nine vessels have no unbudgeted spending at all — V1001, V1003, V1004, V1006, V1007, V1009, V1010, V1012, V1014 — and both sheets agree on the nine. With roughly 730 transactions per vessel over 31 months, there is not a single non-budgeted entry on any of them. That is not credible as a description of real operations: over two and a half years, every vessel eventually needs something that was not planned for.
  • Of the nine vessels without non-budgeted spend, seven are present in the deferred sheet and two are not — so the flag says nothing about whether a vessel was assessed for deferred work.
Decisions

Off_Budget_USD is excluded from all analysis. It is a fraction of budget drawn at random.

Budget_Status is not used to measure unplanned spending. If forced to choose between the two, it is the better-grounded figure.

This gives management a view on fleet-wide control aimed at these two categories:

  • earlier escalation when either runs over

The Handover_Risk column does not match the exposure figures it sits next to. V1004, with the second-most deferred exposure, is labelled Low. V1005, with the second-least, is labelled High.

What this means

Something other than the variance report flagged these vessels — or they were selected for reasons the workbook does not record.
